Inside the studio

A dark loom, cyan warp lines, yellow heddles, and a red shuttle. The white draft at the top is the pattern you must reproduce — row by row.

How a pass works

Think like a weaver. Read the draft, prepare the shed, then let the shuttle travel. Taps are limited, so plan before you touch a thread.

Read the squares

Yellow cells mean those warps should sit high. Dark cells mean those warps stay low for the coming weft.

02

Tap to raise or lower

Each cyan thread is a warp. Tap it to flip its heddle. Raised threads hold the weft above them; lowered threads let it slip underneath.

03

Watch the red shuttle

When the shed matches the next required row, the shuttle crosses and a new line of cloth appears. The draft then asks for a different setup.

04

Spend taps with care

Later patterns add more threads and force repeated state changes. Prepare the whole row before the shuttle moves — wasted taps end the attempt.

What the loom asks of you

Short sessions, rising thread counts, and a pattern that only exists if every pass is exact.

Thread control

You operate a traditional loom: individual warps, not a paint bucket. Every lift and drop is a decision the cloth will remember.

Changing sheds

The required arrangement is never static. After one successful pass, the next row wants a new combination of raised and lowered threads.

Tap economy

Each level gives a tap budget. Flip only what must change. Extra motion is cloth you never get to weave.

Growing drafts

Early boards use a handful of warps. Later designs stretch the loom and demand several state changes before the shuttle is allowed across.

Studio tools

Jump in from Play Now, browse Level Select, pause mid-pass, and spend coins on power-ups when a stubborn row needs help.

Quiet focus

No story dump. Just neon threads, a glowing shuttle, and the satisfaction of a pattern that finally matches the draft.

About the game

Cloth, one shuttle at a time

Loom Weave Puzzle is a weaving logic game. You stand at a compact digital loom and reproduce a target pattern by setting warp threads before each weft pass.

There is no account to create and no social feed to fill. You read the draft, move heddles, and build fabric. If you enjoy patient spatial puzzles and the feel of a real loom — lift, lower, send the shuttle — this studio is set up for you.
